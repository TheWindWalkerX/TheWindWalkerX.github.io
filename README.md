## 🤔 What is this

Every morning, Morning Brief Ultra Pro Max Tier looks at your Google Calendar, your Gmail, and your Google Tasks, and writes you **one page** that says what your day looks like.

That's it. That's the whole thing.

Is it overkill? **Yes.**
Could you have just opened three tabs? **Also yes.**
Did we do it anyway? **One does not simply open three tabs.**

## 😤 Why does this exist

Because at 6:45 AM your brain is a potato 🥔, and the potato has to answer questions like:

- "Do I have a quiz today?"
- "Is that email from the teacher *urgent urgent* or just *regular urgent*?"
- "Why is there a dentist appointment at the same time as chemistry?"

Morning Brief reads all of that *before you wake up* so the potato doesn't have to.

It even catches conflicts. Dentist and chemistry at the same time? It will say so. Loudly. In a little box. Like a hall monitor, but one that is actually helpful.

## ❓ FAQ

**Q: Is this actually "Ultra Pro Max Tier"?**
A: There is no lower tier. There is only Ultra Pro Max Tier. We skipped the rest. Sorry, Basic Tier. You never had a chance.

**Q: Can it read my emails?**
A: It reads the sender, the subject, and Google's short preview, **only with your permission**, **only to show them to you**, and **only on your own computer.** It cannot reply, send, delete, or rearrange anything. Read-only means read-only. We are not like the other apps.

**Q: Does it send my data to some cloud AI?**
A: No. The AI runs locally. Your inbox is not on a trip to anyone's data center. 🏠

**Q: Why does Google show a scary "unverified app" screen?**
A: Because this is a tiny personal project and Google has not reviewed it. That screen is Google's way of saying "are you sure?" And yes, you are sure, because you made it yourself. (If you didn't, close the tab. Seriously.)

**Q: Is this affiliated with Google?**
A: **No.** It is not made, endorsed, or sponsored by Google. It just uses Google's official, documented APIs, like a polite guest who knocked first.

**Q: Will it work on Windows?**
A: It was built for Linux. Windows users can try, but "it works on my machine" has a different meaning there. 🫡

**Q: Can I use it to avoid doing my homework?**
A: It *tells you what your homework is.* Doing it is still, tragically, your department.

**Q: Why is the README longer than the actual website?**
A: Because the website had to be boring and legally useful. This README gets to be free. 🕊️

## 🐛 Known issues

- Mondays. (Cannot fix.)
- If the computer is off at 5 AM, the brief gets made later. It's not late, it's *strategically delayed.*
- The AI sometimes writes sentences that are fine but a little too enthusiastic about chemistry.
- The name is too long. This is a feature.
- Surprised Pikachu face if you forget to re-login. 😮 (Google asks you to sign in again regularly while an app is in testing. It happens. Re-run the sign-in and move on.)

## 🔐 Privacy, but make it honest

The jokes stop here for a second, because this part matters:

- It uses **read-only** permissions for Calendar, Gmail, and Tasks.
- Your data is processed **on your own computer.**
- It is **not** sent to the developer, **not** sold, **not** shared, and **not** used for ads.
- You can remove its access anytime at [myaccount.google.com/permissions](https://myaccount.google.com/permissions).

The grown-up versions of this are on the real pages: [Home](index.html) · [Privacy policy](privacy.html) · [Terms of service](terms.html).
